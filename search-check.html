Can people find Cammack Transport online?

We looked at your website the way Google and a first-time visitor do. Here is what we found, in plain English, with no jargon.

4 of 10

Your site passes 4 of the 10 basic checks. Google can reach your site, and it is quick and secure. Six gaps decide how you look once you are found, and the biggest is the name your site gives itself.

The one that matters most

Your website tells Google its name is "Home".

The title of a page is the blue link people click in a search result. Your home page's title is just "Home". Your other pages are called "Rail Transport", "Palletways" and "Gallery", with no mention of Cammack, Winsford or haulage.

Someone looking for a tachograph centre near Winsford, or a haulier who moves rail components, searches for exactly those words. When a title says "Home", Google most likely rewrites it, or shows a competitor whose title says what they do.

A new site would give every page a title that says it plainly: Cammack Transport, rail and pallet haulage and a commercial vehicle workshop in Winsford, Cheshire.

Five smaller things to fix

All five are quick to put right.

To fix

The description for Google is a list of keywords

The two lines Google may show under your name read "logistics, transportation, pallet, Pallets, trucks, tail lift, delivery, European transportation". It is a list rather than a sentence, and it leaves out your rail work and your workshop.

A new site would write two plain lines: specialist rail haulage, Palletways pallet delivery, and vehicle maintenance and tachographs, from Winsford, Cheshire.

To fix

Shared links show a 2018 award badge

When someone shares your site, the picture that goes with it is your Palletways Platinum Club 2018 badge, the title is your web address and the description is the same keyword list.

A new site would attach a photograph of your fleet and one clear line to every share.

To fix

The home page has no main heading

Every page should have one main heading that says what it is. It is the first thing a search engine reads. Yours has none; the largest words are "Welcome To Cammack Transport Ltd.", which says who you are but not what you do.

A new site would open with a heading that says it: rail haulage, pallet delivery and vehicle maintenance from Winsford.

To fix

Your photographs are not described

Pictures can carry a short written description, used by image searches and read aloud to people who cannot see the screen. On your home page each picture is described only as "Picture", and the fleet photographs in the slideshow have no description at all.

A new site would describe each one in plain words: the fleet at a rail depot, the workshop, the service support van.

To fix

Google is not handed your details

A site can give Google a small fact sheet about the business: what it does, its address and its phone number. Yours does not have one.

A new site would include it, so your number and address show up reliably when someone searches for you.

Can AI assistants find you?

More people now ask ChatGPT, Claude or Google's AI answers instead of searching. These tools read your website too. We checked four things they rely on. Your site passes 3 of 4.

Working

AI assistants are allowed in

Nothing on your site turns them away.

Working

The words can be read without running the page

Your words are there in the page itself, so AI tools can read them.

Working

Your phone number and address are written out

Your phone number and postcode are written on your home page.

To fix

A summary file for AI tools

There is none. It is a newer idea, a short plain-text summary of your three services, which number and extension to ring for each, and where you are, and it takes about twenty minutes to write.

There are only about 160 words on your home page. The more your site says in words about the rail work, the postcodes you deliver pallets to and the workshop, the more an AI tool has to go on.

Nobody can make an AI recommend a business, and anyone who offers to is selling nothing. What a website can do is let these tools reach it, read it and get your facts right.
